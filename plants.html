Underwater Plant Guide

Mastering the art of submerged growth and aquascaping.

Foundation & Setup

Substrate Choice

Aquasoil: Nutrient-rich, lowers pH. Best for heavy root feeders.

Inert Sand/Gravel: No nutrients; requires root tabs but is easier to clean and "cap" other layers with.
Be careful with colorful gravel as it may contain toxic dyes.

Aquasoil Leaching

New Aquasoil often "leaches" high amounts of Ammonia for the first 4-6 weeks. This is great for cycling a tank but dangerous for livestock. Perform frequent water changes during this phase.

The Sand "Cap"

You can either use a 1–2 inch (3–6 cm) layer of sand over nutrient-rich soil to help keep nutrients trapped in the substrate and out of the water column, reducing the chance of algae growth, or use sand alone with root tabs added for plant nutrition. Both methods also create a clean, natural aesthetic.

Planting Technique

Tweezers: Use long aquascaping tweezers to grab stems at the base and push them deep into the soil at a slight angle.

Weights: Lead-free plant weights can help keep "bouncy" plants like Hornwort down until they root.

The Water Column

Plants like Moss and Ferns don't have traditional roots; they eat directly from the water. For these, use liquid fertilizers. For "Rosette" plants (Swords/Crypts), use root tabs in the substrate or aquasoil (this will give nutrients for up to 6 months or even a year, then start adding root tabs).

Some people also use pond substrates as an alternative to traditional aquarium substrates.

Carpet Plants (The Foreground)

▼

✨ Pro Tip: The Dry Start Method

For the best results with carpet plants, try planting them in damp soil without filling the tank for 4-6 weeks. Keep the tank covered with plastic wrap and mist daily. This allows the tiny roots to anchor and spread across the substrate without competing with algae or floating away.
