Water Chemistry Simplified

Think of the water as the "air" your fish breathe. If the chemistry is right, everything else follows.

The Golden Rule

In fishkeeping, Stability is more important than "Perfect" numbers. Most fish can adapt to a wide range of pH or Hardness, but they will get sick if those numbers swing wildly every day. Don't chase the numbers; chase the stability.

Toxic Ammonia (NH₃) Aim for: 0 ppm Analogy: The Poison

Produced by fish waste and rotting food. Any detectable amount burns fish gills and causes stress. In a "Cycled" tank, bacteria should eat this instantly.

Toxic Nitrite (NO₂⁻) Aim for: 0 ppm Analogy: The Suffocator

The middle step of the Nitrogen cycle. Nitrite prevents fish blood from carrying oxygen. Even a small amount can be fatal very quickly.

Safe-ish Nitrate (NO₃⁻) Aim for: 5–40 ppm Analogy: The Leftovers

The final stage of the cycle. It is much less toxic. Plants love it as food! If it goes above 40 ppm, do a water change to bring it down.

Essential Carbonate Hardness (KH) Aim for: ~5 dKH Analogy: The Shock Absorber

This is the "Buffer." It prevents your pH from crashing. Think of it as a shield that protects your water from becoming acidic suddenly.

Essential General Hardness (GH) Aim for: ~10 dGH Analogy: The Vitamins

Measures the dissolved Calcium and Magnesium. Essential for fish bone growth and shrimp shell health. Most tap water is perfectly fine here.

Essential pH Level Aim for: 6.5 – 7.5 Analogy: The Acid Balance

Measures how acidic or alkaline the water is. 7.0 is neutral. As long as it stays steady, your fish will likely be happy.

Standard Temperature Aim for: 25°C (77°F) Analogy: Metabolic Speed

For tropical fish, 23–26°C is the sweet spot. Higher temps speed up metabolism (and waste), while lower temps slow them down.

✨ The "Invisible" Danger: TDS

Total Dissolved Solids (TDS) measures *everything* in the water (minerals, salts, waste). While not a specific toxin, a high TDS usually means the water is getting "old" or "dirty." If your nitrates are low but your TDS is climbing, it's still time for a water change!
