Water Chemistry Simplified

Think of the water as the "air" your fish breathe. If the chemistry is right, everything else follows.

The Golden Rule

In fishkeeping, Stability is more important than "Perfect" numbers. Most fish can adapt to a wide range of pH or Hardness, but they will get sick if those numbers swing wildly every day.

Toxic Ammonia (NH₃) Aim for: 0 ppm Analogy: The Poison

Produced by waste. Even detectable amounts burn fish gills. Bacteria must eat this instantly in a cycled tank.

Toxic Nitrite (NO₂⁻) Aim for: 0 ppm Analogy: The Suffocator

Prevents fish blood from carrying oxygen. Small amounts are fatal very quickly.

Safe-ish Nitrate (NO₃⁻) Aim for: 5–40 ppm Analogy: The Leftovers

Final stage. Less toxic. Plants love it! Keep under 40ppm via water changes.

Essential KH (Carbonate Hardness) Aim for: ~5 dKH Analogy: The Shock Absorber

Prevents pH crashes. Think of it as a shield that protects your water from becoming acidic suddenly.

Essential GH (General Hardness) Aim for: ~10 dGH Analogy: The Vitamins

Measures Calcium/Magnesium. Essential for fish bones and shrimp shells.

Essential pH Level Aim for: 6.5 – 7.5 Analogy: The Acid Balance

Stability is key here. As long as it stays steady, most fish will thrive.

How to Adjust Your Parameters

▼

Increasing pH / KH / GH

KH & pH: Add Crushed Coral or Aragonite to your filter bag. It dissolves slowly, providing a natural, stable buffer. Baking Soda increases KH immediately but can cause dangerous swings.

GH: Use specialized mineral additives (like Seachem Equilibrium) or decorative limestone rocks.

Decreasing pH / KH / GH

The Best Way: Mix your tap water with RO/DI (Reverse Osmosis) or Distilled water. This dilutes the minerals perfectly.

Natural Methods: Peat Moss in the filter or active Aquasoils will naturally "pull" minerals out of the water, softening it and lowering pH over time.

⚠️ A Note on Chemicals

Products like "pH Down" or "pH Up" are often temporary. They fight against your water's natural KH. Once the chemical wears off, the pH will "bounce" back, which stresses or kills fish. Always use natural buffers or RO water instead.

Tannins & The Blackwater Effect

▼

What are Tannins?

Tannins are organic compounds released by decaying plant matter. They turn the water a "tea" or "amber" color. This is highly beneficial for fish from the Amazon or Southeast Asia (Betas, Tetras, Rasboras).

Benefits: Natural anti-fungal/anti-bacterial properties, lowers stress, and slightly lowers pH.

Sources of Tannins

• Catappa Leaves (Indian Almond Leaves)
• Driftwood (Mopani and Malaysian Wood leach the most)
• Alder Cones
• Rooibos Tea (Must be pure, organic, caffeine-free)

How to Remove Tannins

Don't like the "dirty" water look? It's easy to fix:

• Chemical Filtration: Activated Carbon or Seachem Purigen (The best for crystal clear water).
• Water Changes: Regular changes will slowly dilute the color.
• Boiling: Boil new wood for 1-2 hours before adding to the tank to release the "first wave" of tannins.

✨ The "Invisible" Danger: TDS

Total Dissolved Solids (TDS) measures *everything* in the water. While not a specific toxin, a high TDS usually means the water is getting "old." If your nitrates are low but your TDS is climbing, it's still time for a water change!
